## One AP, Two APs, Three APs, Four...

There's a reason nursery rhymes count things up: sometimes you don't know if one will do until you've tried a second, then a third. Wi-Fi in the home is very much like that.

Our story begins with the Fritz!Box 7530 sitting dutifully on the floor, behind the television. It wasn't put there for wireless excellence—it was put there because that's where the fibre installer drilled the hole. From this awkward throne the Fritz!Box rules the airwaves, and sometimes it rules badly. Signals are strong enough in the living room, adequate in the studies, and decidedly weak in the garage and garden.

Then came the question: would a single well-placed access point solve it? The TP-Link WA1201 sat in its box, waiting. Mounted upstairs in the cupboard, high up, centrally placed, it would surely throw better coverage. But was that really true? Or would the house, with its extensions and brick walls, demand a second or even a third AP?

The only honest way to answer is to measure. Wi-Fi is like water: it flows where it can, but pipes and blockages (or in our case walls and chimneys) change everything. You can guess, but unless you walk the property with a meter in hand, you'll never know how many APs you need.

And so begins the test: with one AP, then two, maybe more. Each step revealing whether the bottleneck is not the switch, not the fibre, but the very air between devices.

This is not the glamorous part of networking—there's little prestige in walking about with a laptop, staring at dBm readings—but it's the most honest. And perhaps the most revealing. Because it reminds us that the most expensive switch in the rack means nothing if the Wi-Fi can't keep up.
